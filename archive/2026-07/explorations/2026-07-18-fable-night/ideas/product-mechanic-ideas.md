# Product Mechanic Ideas — Non-AI Family Retention / Ritual / Competition / Physical-World

_Fable-night divergent set, 2026-07-18. Fifteen NEW mechanics (checked against `trials-and-challenges.md` and `pre-mortem-and-backlog.md` — none duplicate the Record Book, Torch Board/torch pass, Co-op Challenge, Tomorrow's Card, Reset Rep, Ghost Bar, Secret Assignment, Rematch Night, Streak Insurance, Apprentice Coach, Beat Dad's Ghost, Squad Consistency Bar, or Family Meter). The test for every devil's-advocate line is the vision's three failure modes: **(1) content exhaustion, (2) consistency collapse, (3) teen drift.**_

Terms per CONTEXT.md. All non-AI mechanics, so client-showcase value is N/A unless noted.

---

## ⭐ TOP 3 (reasons at the end of each)

### 1. Cousin Trials — bring-your-own-opponent family-vs-family ⭐
Two related families (cousins, a friend household, a church-group pair) each run the identical fixed monthly Trials battery in their own garages, and the app shows a **family-vs-family Progress-Points** comparison — the exact size/age-fair currency the Trials doc already mints. This is the *only* between-families competition principle 1 permits, and it needs **zero population liquidity**: you supply your own opponent from a relationship that already exists. It gives the 14-year-old the "competing with others" hook that his drift is *about*, years before a real league, and it turns the founder's own extended-family/church network into the distribution wedge (each Cousin Trials invite is a demo). Family-level scoring only; no individual-minor cross-family comparison.
- **Phase:** 2 (needs multi-family + Squad-adjacent plumbing). **Cost:** M. **Devil's advocate:** cross-family exposure can surface a *weaker cousin kid* to an external peer — the shared-screen-exposure risk (pre-mortem theme 3) at social scale, which is harder to age-gate than in-family; and it couples two families' consistency, so one household's collapse (mode 2) silently kills the other's mechanic.
- **Why top 3:** the sharpest available answer to teen drift (mode 3) that the collaboration-first principle actually allows, and it doubles as the founder's real distribution channel with a population of two families.

### 2. The Comeback, honored as its own line ⭐
Returning from a lapse becomes a *celebrated* beat, not a silent Reset Rep: the Record Book logs comebacks as their own honored stat (longest comeback, most comebacks turned into streaks) and the return session carries a small named ritual — "Day One, again." Consistency collapse is failure mode #1 by observed frequency, and the product's most differentiated stance is the gentle reset; this deepens it by making the *rejoin moment* — the exact point where families are won or lost — feel like an achievement instead of a wound. Cheap, and it compounds the coach-voice missed-week doctrine into a durable surface.
- **Phase:** 1 (extends Reset Rep + Absence Ledger). **Cost:** S. **Devil's advocate:** counting comebacks can perversely *normalize* lapsing ("we're good at coming back") and soften the consistency the product needs; and tallying the gap risks violating coach-voice §4 — the missed week is named once, flat, never quantified. Must celebrate the *return*, never measure the *absence*.
- **Why top 3:** directly targets the highest-frequency failure mode at the precise moment retention is decided, at S cost, without a new subsystem.

### 3. Front Row — grandparent / away-family spectator stream ⭐
A read-only invite ("Front Row") a parent grants to a grandparent, aunt, or the non-training co-parent: they receive the monthly Trials Report and a push on milestone moments only — a torch pass, a first muscle-up, a family-week streak landmark. No live video, no chat, no raw numbers feed (COPPA + scope safe): just the curated milestone stream and the artifact the family already keeps. It raises the stakes for kids (someone who loves them is watching the record grow) and, critically, it hands *felt value* to the often-billing, often-bystander parent (pre-mortem theme 5) and to the extended family who become advocates at renewal.
- **Phase:** 2. **Cost:** M. **Devil's advocate:** an external audience imports performance pressure and comparison the product bans internally — a kid who knows Grandma sees the report may feel exposed (mode 3), and a spectator's absence of engagement could deflate rather than lift. Curation (milestones only, family-level framing) is load-bearing.
- **Why top 3:** it's the cheapest defense against the renewal-churn driver the pre-mortem flagged (value never reaches the payer), and it turns the family's existing love-network into retention gravity.

## The rest

### 4. The Annual — printed year-end Record Book hardcover
A print-on-demand hardcover auto-composed each January from the family's Record Book: every member's tier journey, the year's torch passes, twelve Trials Reports, milestone blurbs. A physical, permanent artifact of a year of training together — and a moat against content exhaustion, because a family cannot "finish" a book that gets a new volume every year. Better than a digital archive: it lives on a shelf and is handed to a kid at 18.
- **Phase:** 2. **Cost:** M (print vendor integration). **Devil's advocate:** it rewards families who *already survived* a year — it does nothing for the month-2 consistency collapse (mode 2) that is where most families actually die, so it's a moat around the wrong wall.

### 5. Chalk It — the physical PB wall
When a PB is set, the app prompts the family to physically record it on a wall surface in their training space (a cling decal or small chalkboard in a welcome kit, or family-supplied). It bridges the authoritative digital record into the garage itself — the record stops being a screen and becomes the room the family trains in, the way real gyms chalk records on the wall.
- **Phase:** 1 (decal) / mechanic. **Cost:** S–M. **Devil's advocate:** it bolts a physical dependency onto a product whose entire value is "open laptop, train" — a missing or full chalkboard becomes friction and low-grade guilt (mode 2), and a hand-kept wall can drift out of sync with the digital Record Book and quietly undermine its authority.

### 6. The Family Break — an authored call-and-response ritual
The family authors a short physical ritual — a chant, a fist-bump sequence, a huddle "break" — and the app cues it (a beat and a prompt) at session open and at the start of every Trials test. Pure ritual scaffolding at zero content cost; it marks the transition into "we train now" and gives the youngest a role in the opening.
- **Phase:** 1. **Cost:** S. **Devil's advocate:** scripted "fun" is exactly what kids see through — a forced chant is the Entertainer anti-pattern in physical form and risks cringe with the 14-year-old (mode 3), even while it delights the 8-year-old. A ritual that splits the family by age is worse than none.

### 7. Founding Day — the anniversary re-enactment
On the family's join anniversary, the planner resurrects the exact Placement Day battery so the family physically re-runs their very first session and sees a full-year delta on every movement. A once-a-year "look how far we've come" ritual with a fixed, earned emotional payoff — the kids literally out-lift the children they were.
- **Phase:** 1 (reuses Rematch Night machinery). **Cost:** S. **Devil's advocate:** an annual hook does nothing for month-2 collapse (mode 2) — the families who reach the anniversary were never the at-risk ones — and it partly overlaps the already-adopted Rematch Night / Then-vs-Now Recap; the marginal ritual value over those is thin.

### 8. Iron Season — chapters over an endless stream
Group the training year into named seasons (a sports-season shape) with a deliberate opener and a finale event, so the endless generated stream gains beginnings, arcs, and endings to anticipate. This attacks content exhaustion (mode 1) at the *emotional* level the pre-mortem didn't: infinite variety can feel shapeless, and shape is what sustains anticipation across months.
- **Phase:** 2. **Cost:** M. **Devil's advocate:** seasons impose a calendar and an arc onto a product whose core strength is bending to the family's life — a "season finale" a busy family misses becomes a fresh rigidity failure (mode 2), and the framing edges toward the entertainment tone the coach voice bans.

### 9. Invite a Witness — the hosted Trials
Extend the Judge role to a real guest: a grandparent, a family friend, or a visiting relative can be named the official judge/counter for a Trials night, turning the monthly benchmark into a hosted event with a witness who isn't in the family. It raises the ceremony's stakes and gives an outsider a genuine, bounded role (hold the counter) rather than passive spectating.
- **Phase:** 2. **Cost:** S. **Devil's advocate:** a witness makes the Trials a *performance*, and a kid having a bad test night in front of a guest is the exposure failure (mode 3) with no easy exit; it also adds coordination overhead to a ritual whose flexibility (splittable, grace weekend) exists precisely because families are busy (mode 2).

### 10. Birthday Benchmark — the annual age line
On a member's birthday the app offers an optional age-anchored test that logs a lifelong birthday record line (push-ups at 9, at 10, at 11…), building an inherently age-fair "you versus your birthdays" ledger that recurs on the one calendar date nobody forgets. It plants a durable, self-comparing benchmark that grows more meaningful every year.
- **Phase:** 1. **Cost:** S. **Devil's advocate:** a rep test on a celebration day can read as homework, and an arbitrary "reps = your age" gimmick cuts against the athletic-respect / universal-standard principle (mode 3, the teen smells the gimmick); annual cadence also does nothing for month-2 collapse.

### 11. Open Garage — the guest session
A family can bring a guest into a session (a visiting cousin, a kid's friend) as a temporary, un-tracked participant who gets a one-off scaled slice. Training becomes something you *host*, which is both a bonding ritual and the product's most natural viral on-ramp — the guest family experiences the coach live, in a friend's living room, with zero marketing.
- **Phase:** 2. **Cost:** M. **Devil's advocate:** a non-member minor in the session raises real COPPA questions and planner/roster complexity, and "hosting" cuts against the family-only surfaces the compliance model deliberately keeps closed — the growth upside may not survive the privacy posture.

### 12. Family Crest — the earned coat of arms
The family builds a crest at Placement Day that appears on every artifact (lobby, Trials Report, share card), and it *evolves* — gaining elements as the family hits collective milestones (a bar element for the first muscle-up, a star for a full year of streaks). It's the family's own visual identity, earned collectively, never bought — an anti-content-exhaustion surface because it keeps changing as long as they train.
- **Phase:** 2. **Cost:** M (design system + optional print). **Devil's advocate:** an evolving badge is a cosmetic reward, and the coach-voice doc bans the XP-dispenser precisely because kids see through earned-token gamification (mode 3); a crest risks reading as a +500-XP sticker and cheapening the athletic-respect positioning.

### 13. The Job Board — rotating non-athletic roles
A rotating family job board of non-training roles — the Judge, the Scribe (does the Chalking), the MC — so a kid who isn't training a given night still has a real reason to be in the room. It keeps the youngest engaged between turns and gives an off-night member a way to stay inside the family system rather than drifting out of it.
- **Phase:** 1. **Cost:** S. **Devil's advocate:** legitimizing a non-training role can *license* not-training ("I'm just the scribe tonight"), eroding the participation the Pact exists to protect (mode 2); it also overlaps the already-adopted Apprentice Coach and the Judge role, so the marginal new value is small.

### 14. Family Motto — the family's own words
At Placement Day the family writes a one-line training creed in *their own words*, and it renders on the lobby and the Trials Report — the one voice on those surfaces that isn't the coach's. A cheap identity anchor that makes the app feel like *this* family's, deepening the coach-voice "knows this family" superpower with the family's own contribution.
- **Phase:** 1. **Cost:** S. **Devil's advocate:** a motto is set-and-forget — near-zero ongoing retention pull once written — and a self-authored creed risks cringe that ages badly, especially for the teen (mode 3) who didn't pick it at 10.

### 15. The Torch Totem — a physical traveling object
A single physical object (a marked pull-up-bar grip, a carabiner, a token) physically lives with whoever currently holds a chosen family record; when a torch pass fires, the app declares the handoff and the object moves. It turns the digital Torch Board into a household totem — tactile, coveted, contested at the dinner table.
- **Phase:** 2 (ship in a kit) or S if family-supplied. **Cost:** S. **Devil's advocate:** a single trophy makes competition *individual and in-family* — a direct hit against principle 1 (inside the family it's collaborative; competition only between families or your own past). A totem the 14-year-old always holds crushes the 8-year-old and drives him out (mode 3 inverted onto the youngest), the opposite of keeping them training together.

## Fence-compliance check (self-audit)

- **No runtime LLM in the workout loop:** all fifteen are deterministic/UI/physical mechanics; none add a live model call. The Annual, Front Row, and Trials-Report surfaces reuse the existing Tier-2 *async* artifact path — no new in-session inference. ✅
- **45-min cap / bodyweight-pure / no gender in data model:** none add exercises, duration, equipment, or gendered schema. Cousin Trials, Birthday Benchmark, and Landmark-style volume ideas were deliberately kept off any mechanic that would tempt session-padding past the cap. ✅
- **COPPA:** Front Row is milestone-stream + report only (no raw minor feed, no chat, family-level framing); crest/motto/totem carry no minor PII; Open Garage (#11) is the one idea that **touches the COPPA fence** — its devil's-advocate flags it, and it is marked requires-review, not adopted. ✅ (with #11 flagged)
- **Founder reviews every library item:** none introduce unreviewed generated content; mottos/crests are family-authored inputs, not library items.
- **Offline-during-workout:** all in-session beats (Family Break #6, Chalk It #5 prompt, Job Board #13) are local UI cues; no network dependency mid-session. ✅

## Open founder questions

1. **Physical fulfillment appetite:** The Annual (#4), Chalk It kit (#5), and Torch Totem (#15) each graft a logistics business onto 8-hrs/week software. Is *any* physical fulfillment in scope pre-Phase-2, or should all physical-world mechanics stay "family-supplied / print-it-yourself" until there's a fulfillment partner? (Recommendation: family-supplied only until Phase 2; The Annual is the one worth a real vendor.)
2. **Between-family competition timing:** Cousin Trials (#1) is the strongest teen-drift answer but lands in Phase 2. Is there a Phase-1 manual version worth piloting inside the founder's own extended family (two garages, shared Trials battery, results compared by hand) as gate-adjacent validation?
3. **The exposure line:** Front Row (#2), Invite a Witness (#9), and Cousin Trials (#1) all add an external audience to a product that carefully bans internal comparison. Where is the founder's comfort line on *outsiders* seeing a specific kid's numbers — and does the under-11-private default (theme 3) extend to spectators automatically?
