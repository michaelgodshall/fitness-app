# Product Vision & Principles

_Foundation doc v2.0 — July 2026. Supersedes `docs/legacy/01-vision-principles.md` (Oct 2025). Written after 16 months of real family training and a full re-visioning session; the legacy docs remain as reference._

---

## Mission

**Help parents and kids get genuinely strong together — with an AI coach that makes family training impossible to outgrow and easy to keep doing.**

Fitness is the vehicle; the relationship is the destination. Every product decision answers to one question: *does this keep the family training together?*

---

## The ground truth (why we know this works — and where it breaks)

This product is built on 16 months of OBSERVED reality: a father training with his sons (now 9, 10*, and 14) using adapted bodyweight programs. That experience validated the core magic **and exposed exactly three failure modes** — which are the product's reason to exist:

**What worked (protect these):**
- **Relative loading scales automatically.** A 70-lb kid doing push-ups faces the same relative challenge as his 180-lb dad. One workout genuinely serves all ages — no "kid version," minimal modifications (knee push-ups, inverted rows — that's nearly the whole list).
- **Shared struggle bonds.** Suffering through the same AMRAP creates connection that passive activities don't.
- **~45 minutes is the ceiling.** Every program that ran longer got hand-trimmed to fit. Kids' focus and family schedules agree.
- **Athletic seriousness engages kids.** They rise to real training; they see through entertainment-fitness.

**What failed (the product must solve these — not just track them):**
1. **Content exhaustion.** The family completed its programs (one of them twice) and excitement died. Fixed program libraries — including the legacy plan's own — carry a built-in expiration date.
2. **Consistency collapse under real life.** Busy months broke the habit. A program that demands *its* schedule loses to a life that has its own.
3. **Teen drift.** The 14-year-old outgrew the ceiling — wanting harder skills, weights, independence, and "training toward something or competing with others." He drifted to solo training.

## The product

**An adaptive family training coach.** Open the app, tell it who's training and how much time you have, and it builds tonight's session — balanced against the week's training history, tuned per member, capped at 45 minutes, with a "beat your best" moment when one is earned. It is structurally incapable of running out of program, and it bends to the family's real life instead of demanding the reverse.

Determinism lives exactly where trust and comparability matter:
- **Monthly Trials** — a fixed 5-test battery (push/pull/legs/core/endurance); the family's stable benchmark.
- **Challenges** — fixed max-effort events, earned through consistency.
- **Skill progressions** — fixed, aspirational paths (muscle-up, handstand push-up, pistol squat, front lever) that give older kids something to train *toward*.

Generation lives where variety matters: the daily session. Under the hood (v1): a deterministic planner assembles sessions from a founder-reviewed exercise/block library inside hard constraints; LLMs generate library candidates in reviewed batches and power the coach's voice. No AI improvisation stands between a family and a workout.

**Bodyweight-pure in v1** (a doorway pull-up bar is the only equipment, ever, for v1). The architecture stays open to dumbbell/hybrid programs later — but the foundation defends the scaling magic.

## Who it's for

- **Primary:** intentional fathers (30-50) training with kids in the **8-14 sweet spot**, with engagement designed to stretch to ~16 via skills and (later) competition.
- **Product schema is parent-child generic** — nothing in the app assumes gender or role ("What do you want to be called? Dad, Mom, Coach, Papa…"). The father-son focus lives in brand, story, and distribution, not in the data model.
- **Family shapes:** 1-4 members training on a shared screen; a teen companion mode (13+, parent-granted) arrives in Phase 2 so solo teen sessions still feed the family system.

## Principles

1. **Relationship-first.** Family achievement over individual metrics. Inside the family it is always collaborative; competition (later) happens *between* families or against your own past self.
2. **One workout, all ages.** Relative loading + per-member difficulty swaps. Never separate "kid programs."
3. **Athletic respect.** Adult terminology, universal standards, real measurement. Kids are athletes, not an audience.
4. **45 minutes, never more.** Progression through density, never duration.
5. **The workout bends to your life.** Time-adaptive, roster-adaptive session generation. A 25-minute Tuesday with two of three kids is a *success path*, not a compromise.
6. **Never stale, always a target.** No two sessions identical — except strategic repeats engineered to create "beat your best" moments.
7. **Benchmarks are sacred.** Trials, Challenges, and skill standards never vary. Generated variety everywhere else is what makes fixed benchmarks meaningful.
8. **Consistency over perfection.** The weekly Pact rewards showing up; missed weeks reset gently, never shame.
9. **Training toward something.** Every member always has a visible next: a PB, a tier, a skill unlock. Especially the teens.
10. **Equipment minimalism.** Start with zero. One pull-up bar unlocks everything in v1.
11. **Safety without softness.** Plain-spoken max-effort rules ("Pain ≠ fatigue. Pain = stop."). Athletic tone, real protection, zero patronizing.

## North star

**Families sustaining 3+ shared sessions per week, month after month.**

Success in one sentence: *a dad opens the app on a chaotic Tuesday with 30 minutes and two kids, and the coach makes tonight work — again — until the streak is the family's identity.*

The first family it must work for is the founder's own. That is Phase 0's gate, and it is not negotiable.
